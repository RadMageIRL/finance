# Changelog

All notable changes to RadMage Finance are recorded here.

## 0.1.0 (2026-11-16)

### Added

- Site foundation: a static Astro site served under `/finance/`, with TypeScript, hand-written CSS and no client framework.
- Theme system: Dark by default, with Light and System options. The choice is saved on the device, applied before the first paint, and System follows the operating system live. Without JavaScript the site is dark.
- Responsive header with Home, News, Learn, Calculators and a More menu (About, Methodology, Sources, Corrections, Privacy, Disclaimer), a phone menu, breadcrumbs and a footer. More works without JavaScript.
- News section preview at `/finance/news/`: the layout for Markets, Economy, Federal Reserve, Banking, Crypto and Regulation, with feed status, category links, a story layout key and related-coverage grouping. No stories are collected yet, nothing is invented, and the page is not indexed until feeds are live.
- A Financial News section on the homepage linking into each news category.
- Calculation engines, kept separate from the pages: money rounding (half away from zero, with interest postings computed exactly), rate and APY conversions, Regulation DD APY and APY earned, the Regulation DD worked examples (reproduced with an inferred crediting assumption that the regulation does not require), compound interest, loan payments and amortization schedules with extra and one-time payments, and a balance-sheet ledger with the transaction catalog and its accounting invariants. Invalid or unrepresentable input is reported, never computed.
- An independent reference oracle in Python using decimal arithmetic, with fixtures for every published Regulation DD worked example, rate conversions, full loan schedules and compound interest tables. The engines match it to the cent.
- Test-integrity audits: a check that test names match their assertions, and a mutation pass that perturbs every rounding rule, threshold and formula constant.
- Compound interest calculator at `/finance/calculators/compound-interest/`: starting balance, rate, compounding frequency, years, and contributions (none, weekly, biweekly, monthly, quarterly or annually, independent of compounding) at the start or end of each interval, on a stated calendar with real month lengths; a results breakdown that adds up to the cent, a year-by-year chart with a hover and keyboard tooltip and a table equivalent, CSV download, and share links that keep the scenario in the part of the address after #, with exact, rounded or no dollar amounts and a plain statement of what such a link does not keep private. The example scenario is calculated at build time, so results show without JavaScript.
- APR and APY calculator at `/finance/calculators/apr-apy/`: converts a savings account's interest rate to its APY or an APY back to the rate for annual to daily compounding, compares up to three accounts with the interest each would pay on a chosen balance, notes when rounded APYs hide a difference, shows how compounding changes the APY for the same rate, and explains, with citations to Regulation DD and Regulation Z, why a savings APR and a loan APR are different measures. Chart with a table equivalent, CSV download and share links.
- High-yield savings calculator at `/finance/calculators/savings-growth/`: projects a savings balance from its advertised APY the way banks figure interest (a daily rate of 1/365 of the interest rate on each day's balance, compounded daily or monthly, credited monthly and rounded to the cent), with regular deposits on the shared calendar, up to three rate changes, the interest rate each APY implies, and the Regulation DD APY earned each month. The conventions Regulation DD leaves to each bank (the rate implied by the APY, rounding, the calendar, when a deposit starts earning, monthly crediting) are each labeled as an assumption. Chart, schedule, CSV and share links.
- Reports and schedules for every calculator. A schedule section shows a chart, a table or both, grouped by day, month or year whatever the compounding: opening balance, deposits, interest accrued (unrounded, to a hundredth of a cent), interest credited (only where a crediting period ends), rate or APY, closing balance and cumulative interest, and the APY earned by month on the savings calculator. Day and month rows come from the engines' own crediting periods, never from dividing totals, and every grouping reconciles to the same final balance. Long tables are paged (a year of days per page). Exports, all made in the browser and never uploaded: a self-contained HTML report (brand, calculator, date, inputs, assumptions, summary, interest versus what was put in, rate changes, chart and full schedule; no scripts, fonts or requests, with a policy that forbids them), the schedule as CSV (every row, even 36,500 days), a plain-text summary, and a print view for Save as PDF that starts the schedule on a new page. File names never contain an amount. Reports say plainly that they are hypothetical calculations, not statements or advice.
- APR and APY calculator: a balance illustration that grows a single deposit in each account period by period, by day, month or year, for up to 30 years, with the assumptions stated.
- Charts by month and day draw the balance as stacked areas, the APR and APY illustration as labeled lines, and every chart adds hatching in print and in forced-colors mode so its series never rely on color alone.
- The independent oracle now also checks every day, month and year of the schedules and every row of the rate illustration.
- Loan amortization calculator at `/finance/calculators/amortization/`: the monthly payment on a fixed-rate loan, the payoff date, total interest and the full schedule by payment or by calendar year, with principal and interest charted apart.
- Mortgage payoff calculator at `/finance/calculators/loan-payoff/`: the same loan with and without an extra monthly payment and up to three one-time payments, side by side: payoff date, payments, total interest, interest saved and time saved, with the balance of both charted. Property tax, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance and HOA dues sit in a separate panel and never enter the loan. Both loan calculators have the full report, CSV, summary, print and share-link options.
- Lesson: How Banks Create Money at `/finance/learn/how-banks-create-money/`. Step through a loan on balance sheets that always balance: the loan creates a deposit, the borrower's payment settles between banks in reserves, the first monthly payment (from the amortization engine) repays principal and interest, a default is written off, a bigger loan runs into a reserve shortfall that stops the simulation with labeled choices (borrow from the other bank, borrow from the discount window, or cancel), and a simplified capital rule refuses a loan. Capital, liquidity and credit risk are each named, with the real US rules cited; the money multiplier appears as a labeled historical model. A free mode lets you post your own transactions.
- Lesson: How Money Flows at `/finance/learn/how-money-flows/`. Households, a business, two banks, the Treasury, the Federal Reserve, an investor and a money market fund: guided steps through wages, spending, saving, lending, repayment, a payment between banks, taxes, government spending, a Treasury security sale and a Federal Reserve purchase, then a free mode with the whole transaction catalog. A flow view (who paid whom) and the balance sheets stay in step, with totals for money, deposits, reserves and the Treasury General Account.
- Lesson: What Is Money? at `/finance/learn/what-is-money/`. The three textbook functions of money, commodity and fiat money, money as a promise (whose liability each kind is), and a comparison of cash, bank deposits, central bank reserves, Treasury bills, money market fund shares, payment stablecoins, bitcoin and similar crypto assets, and gold: whose promise each is, its legal status, who can hold it, how a payment settles, insurance or backing, and its price against the dollar. Compare up to three side by side or read every answer to one question; the full comparison is on the page without JavaScript. Every fact cites a primary source (statutes, the Federal Reserve, the FDIC, the Treasury, the SEC, the CFTC, the IRS, the GENIUS Act and its rules) checked on November 15, 2026, with time-sensitive facts dated.
- A reusable calculator kit: labeled inputs that accept pasted amounts like $1,234.56, results that update after a short pause in typing and are withdrawn the moment an entry becomes invalid, inline error messages, an SVG chart drawn at its real width so labels stay readable on phones, a sticky result bar on phones, and exact formatting and CSV helpers.
- Learn and Calculators section pages listing the first three lessons and five calculators; lessons and calculators that are not built yet are shown as in development and are not links.
- Reference pages: About, Methodology, Sources, Corrections, Privacy and Disclaimer.
- Discoverability: descriptive titles for the lessons; structured data for every page (lessons as learning resources and calculators as free finance applications, both naming RadMage Labs as author and publisher, and breadcrumb trails below each hub); a 1200 by 630 sharing image for each lesson and calculator and a default for the rest, made by `tools/og-images.mjs`; and the review date as `lastmod` in the sitemap. The built-output check now verifies all of these.
- A strict Content Security Policy on every page, no third-party requests and no cookies.
- Local checks: house-style text scan, type check, unit tests, build output checks (links, origins, metadata, sitemap, page weight, words run into links), and browser tests for the theme, accessibility in every theme, layout at 320 px, privacy, and the reports (offline viewing, print layout, CSV values).
- Schedules by year on all five calculators: each year has a button that shows its months underneath (its payments on the loan calculators), one year at a time or with Expand all and Collapse all, by mouse or keyboard. The months are the engine's own month rows, never a year divided by twelve, and each year's month table ends with the year's totals, which match the year's row. On the APR and APY page the months show each account's opening balance, interest accrued, interest credited and closing balance, and how far each later account is from the first (calculation conventions v1.7 define the illustration's accrued interest; the independent oracle checks it). The Chart, Table and Both views and the Day, Month and Year groupings are unchanged. A new export option includes each year's months under the year in the HTML report and the printout and makes the CSV the full monthly schedule, whatever is expanded on the page. The loan month column is now "Regular payment".
- Release tooling: the MIT License for the original application code, a manual GitHub Pages workflow (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`), `tools/dist-manifest.mjs` (SHA-256 of every built file, to compare a deployment with a local build) and `tools/verify-prod.mjs` (read-only checks of a deployment: pages, canonicals, headers, the 404 page, redirects, sitemap and the other RadMage Labs apps).

### Changed

- Privacy: names the services that deliver the pages (Vercel for radmagelabs.com, which fetches them from GitHub Pages) and quotes what each says it logs, with links to their privacy statements.
- How Banks Create Money and How Money Flows: the simulator's 5 percent capital rule is labeled a hypothetical rule for teaching, not a regulatory minimum, in the lesson text, the step, the free-mode switch and its refusal message, and in each lesson's list of simplifications.
- Write-offs: the lessons now say that a charge-off is the bank's accounting entry and does not cancel the borrower's debt (citing the OCC), that the simulation also removes the loan from the borrower's balance sheet as if forgiven, and the free-mode action is named "writes off and forgives a loan". The flow view draws a write-off as the bank writing off the loan instead of as a repayment.
- The money multiplier quote is attributed to its authors at the Federal Reserve Board and the St. Louis Fed instead of to St. Louis Fed economists.
- What Is Money?: the GENIUS Act wording says it takes effect on January 18, 2027 and lists the rules issued so far (the OCC final rule and the Treasury interim final rule, now cited); the other agencies' rules are proposals.
- Loan amortization: amortizing is defined as paying the loan down to zero with every payment repaying some principal (citing the CFPB), not as a level payment.
- Mortgage payoff: HOA dues are listed apart from the mortgage payment, and the total is labeled an estimated total monthly housing cost, on the page and in the report.
- Compound interest, APR and APY, and savings: the export note says a daily view longer than 3,660 rows appears by month in the report and printout while the CSV keeps every day, and the plain-text summary says so when it happens instead of pointing to the HTML report for every row.
- Mortgage payoff: the monthly housing costs now show principal and interest, the escrow items (property tax and homeowners insurance) with their subtotal, mortgage insurance, and an estimated mortgage payment, with HOA dues listed outside it and the estimated total monthly housing cost kept. The page, the report and the summary show the same figures, which the engine computes and the independent oracle checks (calculation conventions v1.6).
- All five calculators label their results "Projection" instead of "Forecast"; Methodology defines a projection as a calculator's result, hypothetical and not a prediction.
- Compound interest: the compounding hint separates adding interest to the balance from crediting it. Mortgage payoff: the extra-payment explanation says an extra payment saves the interest that amount would have been charged over the rest of the loan, and adds that whether paying extra makes sense depends on other financial obligations and the loan's terms. Both loan calculators call the total "Total paid (principal and interest)" and note that many auto loans figure simple interest by the day (citing the CFPB).
- High-yield savings: the introduction names the daily balance method as one of the two methods Regulation DD allows and says banks may credit on other schedules; the calendar assumption no longer claims a size for the leap-year effect and says what each permitted leap-year rule does instead. APR and APY: the chart hint refers to each account's bar, not a year.
- Home, About, the footer and the News preview describe financial news as in development, not as a live service.
- What Is Money?: fiat money's value is explained through acceptance, confidence, legal tender status (which does not oblige anyone to accept cash) and purchasing power, with the Federal Reserve's stable-prices goal, citing Federal Reserve Education, the Federal Reserve Act and two Federal Reserve answers. The gold coin price is quoted in full: market value of the bullion plus the cost of minting, marketing and distribution.
- How Banks Create Money: lending also depends on creditworthy borrowers wanting loans at the prevailing interest rate, which the Federal Reserve influences (citing the St. Louis Fed article).
- Write-offs: the guided step, the free-mode log, the list of balance sheet changes and the flow view ("writes off and forgives") all say the simulated write-off is a charge-off plus forgiveness, and that a real charge-off by itself does not cancel the debt. The simulation itself is unchanged.
- How Money Flows: the flow view now draws payments that have money on one side only, such as loan interest paid to the bank, wages or a security purchase paid by a bank, a Federal Reserve purchase from an investor and a cash withdrawal, instead of showing "No flows" or only the reserves.

- Release candidate editorial pass, checked against primary sources on November 15, 2026:
  - What Is Money?: stablecoin reserves listed as the GENIUS Act states them (including Treasury notes and bonds with 93 days or less to maturity), with holders' priority limited to the required reserves; the 2023 money market fund rules described exactly (temporary gates removed, liquidation gates kept, a mandatory liquidity fee for institutional prime and tax-exempt funds when a day's net redemptions exceed 5 percent); coins identified as Treasury-issued, not Federal Reserve liabilities; M1, M2 and the monetary base described from the Federal Reserve's own definitions; money market deposit accounts distinguished from money market funds; the count of Federal Reserve account holders dated 2021.
  - How Banks Create Money: the first loan payment takes the whole payment out of money in the model, principal and interest alike; the capital minimums are those of all three federal banking agencies, with the community bank leverage ratio option; the primary credit rate is placed at the top of the federal funds target range; the money multiplier is described as a system process and the Bank of England's wording is restored ("can be a useful way"), cited to the original PDF; goods and services are listed among the simplifications.
  - How Money Flows: four free-mode transactions drain reserves, not two; the reverse repo counterparties are quoted in full; reserves move opposite to the Treasury's account unless something else on the Fed's balance sheet moves; the Federal Reserve's deferred asset is explained where the simulation lets its equity fall.
  - High-yield savings: when a deposit starts earning is described from Regulation DD and Regulation CC (credit received, cutoff hours, banking days); deposit timing is stated in full; monthly statements are stated as assumed; the APY earned explanation now says only daily compounding makes a mid-month deposit lower the month's yield; the interest rate column says it comes from the APY; citation details completed.
  - APR and APY: credit unions follow the NCUA's parallel Truth in Savings rules; Regulation Z's loan APR is placed in closed-end credit, with how prepaid finance charges raise the APR; the 0.05 percentage point APY tolerance; the one-year interest uses the APY before display rounding.
  - Compound interest: start and end contribution timing stated exactly, partial final intervals, the textbook formula's contribution term and the Regulation DD daily rate cited.
  - Loan calculators: loan terms may include a prepayment penalty or other restrictions on paying early (citing the CFPB); for an existing loan the computed payment can differ a little from the statement; the regular payment stays the same after extra payments. Schedules by year call the year's column "Total paid", and notes explain Regular payment, Total paid and the CSV columns `payment_usd`, `scheduled_usd` and `extra_usd`.
  - Methodology lists six kinds of statement (it said five after Projection was added), explains accrued versus credited interest and the APR and APY illustration's exception to rounding, and no longer implies regulator examples exist for every calculator; Home, About, Sources and Privacy wording made exact.
  - Source entries updated where pages moved or were renamed (IRS digital assets, the Treasury's gold report, the CFPB PITI answer).
